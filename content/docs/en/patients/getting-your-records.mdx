Your visit notes belong to you. Here's how to actually get them.

## The simple way

Ask your clinician or clinic for a copy. Most will provide the note as a
**PDF** — the finalized, physician-approved document. That's the version
you want for your personal records.

## Machine-readable exports

If you want your data in a format computers can use — to share with
another provider, a personal health record app, or a researcher — ask your
clinic whether they use KTB Health. KTB supports exporting patient records
and visit notes in two open interoperability standards:

- **FHIR R4** — the modern standard used by most health systems and apps.
- **openEHR** — a clinical-modeling standard used in some health systems.

These exports contain your demographics, visit notes, and structured
clinical content — everything the clinician approved, in a format other
software can read.

<Callout type="info" title="Ask for the right thing">
  For personal use, the PDF is usually best. For sharing with other
  providers or apps, ask specifically for a **FHIR** export — it's the most
  widely supported format.
</Callout>

## What you won't get

- **Raw audio** — session recordings are internal pipeline artifacts, not
  part of your record. Your clinic decides retention policy.
- **AI drafts** — the unedited machine drafts are working documents. The
  record is what your clinician reviewed and approved.

## If your clinic doesn't use KTB

The same rights apply — request your records in whatever format they have.
FHIR export is increasingly standard; if they can't provide it, a PDF or
printed copy still satisfies most access requests.
