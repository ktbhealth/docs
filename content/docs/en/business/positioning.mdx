KTB Health occupies a deliberately narrow, defensible position in clinical
software: **ambient documentation for physicians**.

## The problem

Documentation competes with care. In a time-and-motion study of 57 physicians
across 4 specialties in the USA, only **27%** of the office day went to direct
time with patients, while **49%** went to the EHR and desk work: nearly
**2 hours** of documentation for every hour with a patient, plus **1–2 hours**
each night after work.

<Callout type="info" title="Source">
  Sinsky C. et al. *Allocation of Physician Time in Ambulatory Practice: A Time
  and Motion Study in 4 Specialties.* Annals of Internal Medicine,
  2016;165(11):753–760. After-hours figures come from diaries kept by 21 of the
  physicians.
</Callout>

## The one rule

> The physician decides. KTB Health records.

KTB captures the consultation, transcribes it, and structures a draft note.
Everything clinical — interpretation, judgment, the final word — belongs to the
clinician. This isn't a marketing line; it's an enforced product constraint
(tracked as T-021 in the org ledger) that applies to UI copy, AI output, and
documentation alike.

## Why it matters

- **Regulatory clarity.** A documentation tool avoids the diagnosis/
  treatment-device question entirely. KTB is software that records, not a
  system that decides.
- **Physician trust.** Clinicians adopt tools that assist without overruling
  them. Every AI artifact in KTB is labeled as a draft pending review.
- **Honest scope.** We would rather be the best scribe in the room than a
  mediocre diagnostician.

## What this rules out

No diagnosis. No treatment recommendations. No triage scores. No "suggested
next steps" that read as clinical advice. If a feature can't be expressed
without the software appearing to decide, it doesn't ship.

KTB Health never:

- issues a diagnosis;
- decides conduct or treatment;
- tells the physician what to think;
- replaces clinical judgment.

Nothing enters the record without the physician's review and approval.
**Always documentation. Never decision.**

## Where the name comes from

**KTB** comes from the Semitic consonantal root *k-t-b*, "to write": *kataba*
(<bdi dir="rtl">كتب</bdi>) in Arabic and *katav* (<bdi dir="rtl">כתב</bdi>) in
Hebrew. Writing the record, so the physician doesn't have to, is the whole
product.
