KTB Health is an **AI clinical documentation assistant**, not a transcription
tool. The product automates the documentation workflow around the consultation
so physicians spend their time with patients, not on records.

> Let doctors focus on patients, while AI handles clinical documentation.

The positioning rule from [Positioning](/en/docs/business/positioning) still
holds and is enforced in the product: **the physician decides, KTB records**.

## The wedge

**Specialty-first, locale-native clinical scribe.**

Brazil is locale #1, not the product identity. Brazilian Portuguese medical
vocabulary, CID-10/TUSS coding, SUS and *convênio* billing context, and the
way Brazilian consultations actually happen are the go-to-market wedge — the
architecture treats locale and specialty as parameters, not hardcodes.

- **Locale** is a parameter: transcription, anamnesis structure, and copy are
  driven by locale packs, not scattered conditionals.
- **Specialty** is a parameter: documentation templates adapt per specialty
  (psychiatry is the beachhead — see below).

## Beachhead

**Psychiatry.** Long consults, heavy documentation burden, template-rich
anamnesis, and high willingness to pay for time savings. The wedge does not
depend on it — the template does.

## Go-to-market sequence

1. **Telemedicine first.** Remote capture is simpler: the transcription bot
   joins Meet, Zoom, Teams and WhatsApp Web calls, or the physician captures
   the browser tab audio directly. Teleconsultation is the initial focus.
2. **In-person, private practices (*consultórios particulares*).** The same
   ambient capture extends to the consultation room — self-serve B2C for
   individual physicians.
3. **Clinics and hospitals.** Multi-tenant clinics need delegation and group
   workflows; hospitals need the deployment options below. The architecture
   is prepared for this from day one — it is not a rewrite.

## Deployment path

Cloudflare-hosted today; **on-premise and private-cloud are architectural
commitments, not roadmap promises**. Domain logic sits behind ports and
adapters (see [Deployment options](/en/docs/business/deployment-options)), so
swapping D1 for Postgres or R2 for S3 is an adapter, not a rewrite. Hospital
deployments can run fully in the customer's environment, including local
model inference.

## MVP scope

One complete workflow, end to end:

1. Doctor starts a consultation — with explicit patient consent.
2. Audio is captured and transcribed, with doctor/patient speaker separation.
3. An LLM structures the conversation into a clinical note.
4. The doctor reviews, edits and approves.
5. The note is exported — PDF, FHIR R4 Bundle, or openEHR composition.

The structured note uses KTB's locale-native anamnesis (chief complaint,
history, background, allergies, medications, physical exam, hypotheses,
conduct) rather than a generic SOAP template — each section carries
**evidence citations**: the verbatim transcript sentences that ground it. The
AI never invents findings; it shows its sources.

## Differentiators

Against Dragon Copilot, Abridge and Nabla:

- **Brazilian Portuguese medical accuracy** — Whisper decoding is biased with
  a clinical glossary (`initial_prompt`) covering conditions, drugs, exams,
  CID-10 and TUSS.
- **Evidence-cited sections** — every field links to the sentences that
  produced it; hallucination is structurally visible, not silently trusted.
- **Physician-approves-only** — documentation-only positioning is a product
  constraint, not a disclaimer.
- **Interoperability** — FHIR R4 and openEHR exports, not just PDF.
- **Deployment flexibility** — cloud or fully on-premise with local models.

## How we measure value

- Time from consultation end to signed note vs. manual documentation.
- Review time per note (edit distance from draft to approved).
- Transcription accuracy (WER) on real PT-BR consult audio via the eval
  suite.

The doctor always reviews and approves. Consent, encryption and LGPD are
architectural, not aspirational — see
[Security and compliance](/en/docs/business/security-and-compliance).

## Honest scope notes

- **Speaker diarization is post-MVP.** Today speaker separation comes from
  separate audio sources (clinician microphone vs. call/tab/bot audio), which
  is reliable for telemedicine. Single-stream diarization for one-microphone
  in-person capture is on the backlog.
- **In-person capture** is designed for but not yet the primary demo path —
  telemedicine ships first.
