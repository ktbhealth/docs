---
title: Notification delivery
description: Why notifications subscribe after commit, isolate failures, and merge a live stream with a persisted backlog.
icon: RadioTower
---

The notifications pipeline makes three deliberate trade-offs that explain
most of its design: delivery is at-least-once-ish (persist first, stream
second), producers never feel consumer failures, and live updates are a
hint — the database is the source of truth.

## After-commit subscription

`events.subscribeAfterCommit` runs projectors only after the producing
command's boundary commits. Consequences:

- A notification write **cannot roll back** the mutation it describes.
- The producing command **cannot be rolled back** by a notification
  failure — `ingest` catches all causes and logs.
- Ordering is "mutation committed, then notify" — never the reverse.

## Persist, then fan out

`ingest` inserts the row first, then publishes to the `PubSub` live
stream. A client that misses the stream still sees the notification on
next load — `listNotifications` reads the table, and the header merges
stream items over a `STREAM_BACKLOG = 50` catch-up window. Live delivery
is an optimization, not a guarantee.

## Failure isolation everywhere

Both `ingest` and `publishRows` wrap their bodies in
`Effect.catchCause(Effect.logError)` — a bad projector, a DB hiccup, or a
dead subscriber logs and moves on. This is the same philosophy as
[audit's never-fails record](/en/docs/developers/plugins/audit/how-to/record-audit-entries):
side-channel writes must not become failure modes of the primary flow.

## Authz is per-operation

Reads require `notification.read`, mutations `notification.update` —
enforced inside `NotificationsLive` via `requirePrincipalUser`, not in
the UI. The stream itself is user-scoped: `stream(userId)` only carries
that user's rows.
