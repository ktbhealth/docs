title: Ports and adapters
description: Why the product codebase is shaped the way it is.
---

The `product` repo follows a ports-and-adapters (hexagonal) architecture, per
ADR-0001 in the `project` repo.

## The idea

Domain logic lives in Effect services behind **ports** — interfaces that
describe capabilities (transcription sources, calendar, notifications) without
naming a provider. **Adapters** at the edge implement those ports for a
specific vendor: a transcription API, a calendar provider, Cloudflare services.

## Why it matters

- Swapping a provider means writing a new adapter, not rewriting domain code.
- Tests run against in-memory port implementations — no network, no mocks of
  vendor SDKs.
- The boundary is enforced mechanically: an arch-lint policy
  (`tools/arch-policy.mjs` in `product`) forbids plugins and ports from
  importing adapters or touching platform APIs (`node:*`, `Bun`, `process`,
  hardcoded provider literals like `@cf/*` model ids).

## Where it shows up

- `plugins/` — feature plugins that must not import from `apps/`; composition
  happens in `apps/web/src/routes/` via injected `api` objects.
- `infra/` — provider adapters (e.g. `infra/cloudflare`) implementing the
  `@ktb/infra-ports` interfaces, selected at the edge.
